How much does it cost to charge an EV at home?

With the example 60kWh usable battery, adding 50 percentage points stores 30kWh. At 90% charging efficiency, that draws about 33.33kWh from the grid and costs £8.77 at 26.32p/kWh. A full 0–100% charge under the same assumptions costs about £17.55.

How is EV charging cost calculated?

The calculator works out the energy added to the battery, then adjusts for charging losses to estimate the electricity drawn from the grid.

Grid energy = (Battery capacity × Percentage added) ÷ Charging efficiency
Charging cost = Grid energy × Electricity price

Full charge, charging losses and cost per mile

For the example 60kWh usable battery at 90% efficiency, a full charge requires 60 ÷ 0.9 = about 66.67kWh from the grid. At 26.32p/kWh this costs about £17.55. The efficiency is an example input, not a measured loss for every vehicle.

Use usable battery capacity, which may differ from the advertised total. Enter percentage points added: charging from 20% to 80% means 60%, not 80%. Charging efficiency and tariffs can vary; if a session spans different unit rates, calculate each part separately.

To estimate electricity cost per mile, divide charging cost by the miles covered using that energy. The calculator has no mileage input, so it cannot calculate your per-mile result directly. If using vehicle battery kWh per mile, divide by charging efficiency before multiplying by the unit rate; if using measured wall kWh per mile, do not adjust for losses again.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a full EV charge cost at home?

For the example 60kWh usable battery and 90% charging efficiency, a 0–100% charge draws about 66.67kWh and costs about £17.55 at 26.32p/kWh. Battery capacity, losses and your tariff change the cost.

How do I work out EV electricity cost per mile?

Divide the electricity cost for a charging period by the miles driven using that energy. Match energy and mileage periods and include charging losses once. The calculator does not have a mileage input.

Should I use usable or total battery capacity?

Use usable battery capacity where available. Advertised total capacity can include energy buffers unavailable for normal driving, which can distort a percentage-based charging estimate.

Why include charging efficiency?

Some energy is lost between the wall and the battery. Efficiency lets the calculator estimate how much energy must be drawn from the grid.

Should I use my home tariff or public charging price?

Enter the price you actually pay per kWh for the charging session you want to estimate.

Does this include standing charges or charger installation?

No. It estimates electricity consumed for charging only.

Can I model a partial charge?

Yes. Enter the percentage of the battery you expect to add, such as 40% or 60%.
