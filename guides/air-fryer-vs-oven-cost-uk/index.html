Air Fryer vs Oven Cost UK: Which Is Cheaper to Run in 2026?

Compare the electricity needed for your whole meal, including preheating and extra batches, rather than choosing by wattage alone.

Air fryer vs oven: quick answer

For small amounts of food, an air fryer will often use less electricity because it heats a smaller cooking space and can cook for less time. An oven can be more practical for larger meals or multiple dishes cooked together, especially if an air fryer would need several batches.

The cheapest option depends on actual power consumption, cooking time, number of batches and quantity of food. An air fryer is not always cheaper, and rated wattage alone does not determine the final cost.

How much does an air fryer cost to run?

At 26.32p/kWh, the example 1.7kW air fryer costs 44.744p per hour at continuous full power, about 44.7p. Over 25 minutes, it uses 1.7 × (25 ÷ 60) = about 0.708kWh, costing 18.6p.

Use your model’s electrical input power and the full session time in the air fryer electricity cost calculator. A measured average power or session kWh will reflect thermostat cycling better than the maximum rating.

How much does an electric oven cost to run?

The example 2.4kW electric oven costs 63.168p per hour at continuous full power, about 63.2p. For 45 minutes, 2.4 × (45 ÷ 60) = 1.8kWh, costing 47.4p at the same reference rate.

This is a full-power estimate, not a prediction for every cooking session. Oven size, temperature, preheating, settings and thermostat behaviour affect consumption. The oven running cost calculator lets you change power, time and tariff.

Why air fryers can cost less for small meals

Heating a smaller cooking space and completing a suitable meal sooner can reduce total energy use. That makes an air fryer worth comparing for one or two people when the food fits comfortably in one session. Appliance efficiency and the recipe matter too; a smaller basket is not a guarantee of lower consumption for every task.

When an oven may make more sense

For a family meal, consider whether an oven can cook the main dish and sides together. If the air fryer needs repeated batches, its total running time increases. The oven may be more practical and can be competitive on energy for the complete meal, even if its rated power is higher.

Match portions and cooking results. Comparing one air-fryer portion with a whole oven meal does not show which costs less for your household.

Does preheating affect electricity cost?

Yes. Electricity used before the food goes in still counts. Include preheating in the total time or add its measured kWh separately, without counting it twice. Apply the same approach to an air fryer if its instructions call for preheating. Follow the appliance and food instructions rather than changing safe cooking times to match a cost example.

Does cooking in batches change the result?

Using the table’s illustrative assumptions, two 25-minute air-fryer batches at 1.7kW use about 1.417kWh and cost 37.3p. One 45-minute oven session at 2.4kW uses 1.8kWh and costs 47.4p. The gap is now about 10.1p, much smaller than when comparing a single air-fryer batch with that oven session.

Three such air-fryer batches would use 2.125kWh and cost about 55.9p, exceeding that one oven session in this example. These are not measured savings or universal results: assume the chosen batches and oven session prepare the same whole meal. Actual batch times, retained heat, preheating and thermostat cycling can change the comparison.

How to calculate your own appliance cost

1,000 watts = 1kW. Divide minutes by 60 to get hours, then use:

Cost in pence = Power in kW × Time in hours × Electricity price in p/kWh

For the air-fryer example: 1.7 × (25 ÷ 60) × 26.32 = 18.6433…p, rounded to 18.6p. Use unrounded values until the final result. Divide pence by 100 to get pounds.

If you have measured the session’s total kWh, simply multiply kWh by your p/kWh rate; do not multiply by time again. Use a suitable energy monitor only where compatible with the appliance and its instructions. Our appliance energy usage guide explains how to find suitable figures.

Frequently asked questions

Is an air fryer cheaper to run than an oven?

Often for a small meal that fits in one batch, but not always. Compare actual energy for the whole task, including preheating and repeated batches, at the same tariff.

How much does an air fryer cost per hour?

At 26.32p/kWh, an illustrative 1.7kW air fryer costs about 44.7p per hour at continuous full power. Actual cost depends on wattage, thermostat cycling and runtime.

How much does an electric oven cost per hour?

At 26.32p/kWh, an illustrative 2.4kW oven costs about 63.2p for an hour at continuous full power. Actual consumption varies with temperature, settings, preheating and element cycling.

Is an air fryer cheaper for one person?

It can be a good option when one person’s meal fits in a single short session. Compare the actual cooking time and energy with the oven rather than assuming the smaller appliance always wins.

Is an oven cheaper when cooking for a family?

It can be competitive when it cooks several dishes or portions together while an air fryer needs multiple batches. Meal size, appliance efficiency and total session energy determine the result.

Does an air fryer use less electricity than an oven?

It may for suitable small meals because it heats a smaller space and may cook faster. Wattage alone cannot establish energy use: include runtime, number of batches, food quantity and thermostat behaviour.

Does preheating make an oven more expensive to run?

Preheating adds electricity consumption to the cooking session. Count it in the oven total and include any required air-fryer preheating too, so the comparison covers both complete tasks.

Is a dual-basket air fryer still cheaper than an oven?

It depends on the combined actual draw of the active baskets and how long they run. Two baskets may avoid sequential batches, but neither the basket count nor the headline wattage proves it is cheaper. Compare measured total kWh for the same meal.

Methodology and reference rate
