EV Charging Cost at Home UK: Standard vs Off-Peak Tariffs

Estimate how much electricity your car needs from the grid and compare home charging tariffs for the same energy and mileage.

Quick answer

For a 60kWh usable battery, charging from 20% to 80% stores 36kWh. At an illustrative 90% charging efficiency, the wall supplies 40kWh. At 26.32p/kWh that costs £10.53. On an example 8p/kWh off-peak tariff the same energy would cost £3.20, provided the session fits the cheaper period.

Examples use 26.32p/kWh, the Ofgem average unit rate for a standard variable electricity tariff paid by Direct Debit in Great Britain. Regional and individual tariffs vary. Check your bill or our UK electricity unit rate guide.

How to account for charging losses

Grid kWh = usable capacity × fraction added ÷ charging efficiency

For 60kWh × 0.60 ÷ 0.90, the result is 40kWh from the wall. If your charger already reports grid kWh for that session, multiply the measured number directly by your tariff; do not add losses twice.

Off-peak tariff vs standard tariff

An off-peak or EV tariff can make home charging cheaper if you can shift charging into lower-cost hours. Check the exact time window, any eligibility conditions, daytime unit rate and standing charge. See Energy Saving Trust's smart-charging guidance.

If a 40kWh session draws 28kWh at 8p and 12kWh at 26.32p, the total is £2.24 + £3.16 = £5.40 (rounded). Do not assume the entire session will qualify for the cheaper price.

Mixed-tariff cost = sum of each period's kWh × its unit rate

Before switching tariff, compare the total household bill. A higher day rate or standing charge could offset some savings on EV charging.

Can a charge fit into an off-peak window?

A charger delivering an illustrative 7kW from the wall could draw up to 28kWh over four hours if that power is sustained. A 40kWh session at that draw would need about 5.7 hours. Actual draw can be limited by the car, charger, battery temperature, settings or charging taper. Follow your charger and vehicle instructions.

Translate charging cost into cost per mile

If the example 40kWh from the wall supports 150 miles of driving, the electricity-only cost at the reference rate is 7.0p per mile; at 8p/kWh it is 2.1p per mile. The distance is illustrative. Use your own measured wall energy and mileage across the same period for a fair estimate.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to fully charge a 60kWh EV?

About £17.55 at 26.32p/kWh and 90% efficiency, assuming a full 0–100% charge.

Is home charging cheaper at night?

It can be on a tariff with a lower applicable overnight rate. A flat-rate tariff has no time-of-day unit-rate saving.

How much does 20% to 80% add?

60 percentage points of usable battery capacity; for 60kWh, 36kWh stored in the battery.

Is 90% charging efficiency guaranteed?

No. It is an example assumption; actual charging losses vary. Use wall-metered energy if available.

Methodology and further reading

The examples use transparent assumptions, not independently measured appliance or vehicle performance. Costs are calculated before rounding, and exclude standing charges unless explicitly stated. See Ofgem's unit rates, our energy-use guide and appliance cost comparison guide.
